# Landscape and Scene

Use this reference for natural landscapes, cityscapes, architecture, interiors, environmental scenes, and scene-heavy images in any medium.

## Observe

- Scene subject, location type, season, time of day, weather, and environmental condition.
- Foreground, middle ground, background, horizon, vanishing structure, framing elements, and scale anchors.
- Terrain, architecture, streets, vegetation, water, snow, haze, smoke, and reflective surfaces.
- Sky gradient, cloud layers, sun or moon position, practical lights, atmospheric scattering, and visibility distance.
- Dominant, secondary, and accent colors and how they change across depth.
- Mood created by openness, enclosure, density, emptiness, weather, light, and human presence.

## Infer cautiously

Distinguish visible time and weather cues from narrative guesses. If dawn and dusk are genuinely ambiguous, state the ambiguity or provide variants. Do not invent a geographic location, season, or event without evidence.

Use the relevant photography, illustration, or 3D reference for medium-specific treatment. This scene reference supplies environmental structure rather than replacing the medium analysis.

## Compile

Anchor the prompt with the large spatial composition before listing small objects. State atmospheric perspective and color progression across foreground, middle ground, and background when they drive the look. Keep light direction consistent with the sky and cast shadows.
