# Photography

Use this reference for photographs and photo-real reconstructions, including portraits, products, interiors, street scenes, and documentary images.

## Observe

- Subject, action, environment, crop, camera height, viewpoint, and distance.
- Perspective behavior: wide-angle expansion, natural perspective, or telephoto-like compression.
- Focus plane, depth-of-field character, bokeh shape, motion blur, and local sharpness.
- Light source direction, apparent size, hardness, falloff, fill, rim light, practical lights, catchlights, and shadow density.
- White balance, contrast curve, highlight rolloff, black levels, saturation, skin or product color, grain, noise, halation, and sharpening.
- For portraits: pose, gaze, expression, wardrobe, hair, background separation, and retouching level.
- For products: silhouette, viewing angle, surface cleanliness, edge definition, label legibility, reflections, grounding shadow, and backdrop sweep.
- For documentary work: moment, environmental context, available-light feel, motion, imperfection, and observational distance.

Describe visible human appearance without inferring identity or sensitive personal attributes.

## Infer cautiously

Describe a **lens feel** rather than claiming an exact lens. Use terms such as wide, normal, short-telephoto, compressed, shallow-focus, or deep-focus. Exact camera body, lens model, focal length, aperture, shutter, and ISO require metadata or supplied provenance.

If numeric settings would help recreation, present them as optional choices. Prefer a range or a visual objective over false precision.

Do not identify a photographer from appearance alone. Translate the look into lighting, timing, composition, color response, grain, and documentary or editorial traits.

## Compile

Prioritize subject, moment, framing, and light before camera terminology. Include film or sensor character only when visible. Avoid stacking incompatible terms such as hard noon shadows with broad shadowless softbox light unless the image genuinely contains both.
