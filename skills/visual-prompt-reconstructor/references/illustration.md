# Illustration

Use this reference for hand-drawn, painted, graphic, flat, cel-shaded, healing, anime-adjacent, folk, or digitally illustrated images.

## Observe

- Shape language, silhouette simplification, proportions, gesture, and visual rhythm.
- Line presence, thickness, taper, pressure variation, edge cleanliness, and line color.
- Coloring method: flat fills, layered glazing, opaque paint, cel shading, soft rendering, gradients, or textured blocks.
- Brush or tool marks, paper/canvas grain, dry-brush edges, ink bleed, print texture, and deliberate imperfections.
- Value grouping, palette size, color harmony, accent placement, and background treatment.
- Composition, depth cues, overlap, scale, perspective stylization, and focal hierarchy.
- Emotional tone expressed through shape, color, spacing, and mark-making.

## Infer cautiously

Distinguish observable technique from guessed software, brush pack, school, national style, or creator. Do not attribute an artist from visual similarity alone. Express the transferable traits directly.

When the image mixes illustration with photography or 3D, preserve the boundary between the media instead of flattening everything into one style label.

## Compile

Describe the subject and composition first, then line, color application, texture, and mood. Prefer concrete instructions such as "thin warm-gray contour lines with slight pressure variation" over broad labels such as "beautiful hand-drawn style."
